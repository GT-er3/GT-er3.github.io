Research

I study the mechanistic interpretability of large language models, with a focus on activation steering — controlling a model's behaviour by injecting a direction vector into one of its intermediate layers.

A central puzzle in this line of work is that the layer where steering works best varies sharply from one model to the next. Existing methods either search for that layer or predict it from black-box representation statistics, but neither explains why the optimal depth differs. I approach this as a mechanism question, decomposing the injected signal into two terms:

  • Downstream amplification — how much the injected signal is amplified as it propagates toward the output.
  • Direct passthrough — how much of it reaches the readout unchanged.

Across 13 models spanning 0.5B–9B parameters and two gated-activation families (SwiGLU and GeGLU), I find that the amplification ratio decreases with depth (Spearman ρ < 0 in 13/13 models; bootstrap 95% CI excludes zero in 7/13), while the injection peak is deep in every GeGLU model and spreads across shallow, mid, and deep layers in SwiGLU models.

How much of this is the instrument?

A second thread concerns measurement. Replicating the same sweep under each model's own LM-head readout, the two readouts agree on both the peak band and the decay sign in only 6/13 models, and the probe readout misreports the peak of the unblocked injection curve in 5/13. The decomposition is therefore readout-dependent rather than readout-invariant. What it supports is a description, not a prediction, of why the optimal depth is model-specific.
